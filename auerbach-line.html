What this timeline supports — and what it doesn't

1. Auerbach was Allen Park's named consultant before Saline's story even began

The 2024 Carlisle/Wortman contract naming Auerbach specifically as Allen Park's Planning Consultant was signed roughly 14 months before Related Digital's first informal appearance in front of Saline Township's Planning Commission (July 8, 2025). He wasn't a planner who "learned the playbook" in Saline and then brought it to Allen Park — he was already Allen Park's guy the entire time Saline's fight was unfolding.

2. The two cases weren't sequential — they overlapped in real time, repeatedly

Four separate dates land an active Saline event and an active Allen Park event on the exact same calendar day: the day the Saline consent judgment was entered and Solstice's plans landed on Carlisle/Wortman's desk (Oct 15, 2025); the day Auerbach spoke with Saline's litigation counsel by phone and Allen Park held its first, most heavily attended postponement hearing (Jan 8, 2026); the day Saline's board tightened its oversight of Carlisle/Wortman and Allen Park's Planning Commission came within one vote of denying Solstice outright (May 13, 2026); and the day Auerbach presented Saline's moratorium/HEID ordinance in the same meeting Saline announced his firm's phase-out, while in Allen Park Fred Frank emailed him the proposal that became Allen Park's own moratorium ordinance (June 2, 2026).

3. A recurring behavioral pattern, not just a recurring person

In both places the same shape repeats: an early planning judgment favorable to the applicant, direct and largely undisclosed-to-the-public contact with the developer's side at the moments that mattered, and Auerbach personally naming or facilitating the developer's next procedural move rather than the city/township's own attorney doing so.

4. The honest limits of the evidence

The Oct. 1, 2025 Teams call is billed under a separate "Analysis/Strategy" category from the consent-judgment drafting itself — it's not proven the call's subject was the judgment. The executed 44-page consent judgment contains zero mentions of Auerbach or Carlisle/Wortman. And Auerbach's June 23, 2026 email to Solstice's own counsel — asserting the moratorium blocks their ZBA appeal — cuts the other way entirely: that's Auerbach helping Allen Park hold the line, not assist the developer. The fair reading isn't "Auerbach always helps the developer." It's that he's a single, largely invisible node sitting inside both communities' most consequential moments, on both sides of the table, more often than a neutral technical consultant's role would predict.

5. What would turn correlation into something stronger

Two Michigan LLC filings — RD Michigan (Saline's developer) and Solstice LLC (Allen Park's developer) — registering with the state one calendar day apart, Aug 25–26, 2025, is a striking coincidence but not, on its own, evidence the developers are connected. The single most useful next document would be Auerbach's own calendar or detailed timesheet covering July 2025–June 2026.
