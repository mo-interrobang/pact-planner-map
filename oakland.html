Oakland County — Data Center Watch PROVEN vs UNPROVEN

A county-focused companion to the statewide map: what is documented (green), what is circulating unverified (red), when each fight moved, and where every municipality sits. Hover timeline events for detail — click a dot to pin the card and follow its source link.

Compiled 2026-08-24 by P.A.C.T. from official minutes, agendas, court filings, and press — every green card links to its source. Red cards state what evidence would settle them.
✅ Proven — documented and sourced
Proven 1 — Woodward's Sheetz Consulting
The county board chair is a paid consultant for a company with business before local governments
Dave Woodward works as a paid Sheetz consultant through Woodward & Associates LLC while chairing the Board of Commissioners — appearing for Sheetz at Farmington Hills (voted down; Sheetz sued the city) and Royal Oak (approved) — and refuses to disclose his compensation. Crain's · Axios · Detroit News 7/31/25
Proven 2 — The County-Land Lever
"I have been overruled" — the airport easement episode
WXYZ documented Woodward pushing a Sheetz project adjacent to county-owned airport land in Waterford: airport manager Cheryl Bush's internal email says she was "overruled" on an easement she opposed on safety grounds, names Woodward as "a strong proponent," and says he "made introductions to the Sheetz folks." The county declined the easement only after reporters asked. This is the one proven instance of the pattern the golf-course theory imagines — and the template for what to watch on any county-owned land. WXYZ investigation
Proven 3 — Springfield's Planner Flip
CWA hired mid-moratorium, mid-mine-fight — Ben Carlisle pitched it personally
Two steps: Jan 12, 2026 — CWA takes over Building Department functions. Mar 12, 2026 — the board votes 7-0 to fire Giffels Webster and hire Carlisle/Wortman as planner, with Ben Carlisle presenting (citing CWA experience with "gravel mining and data centers") and Megan Masson-Minock as primary consultant, championed by Supervisor Ric Davis. This happened with a data center moratorium, documented developer interest, and the Levy mine application all pending — the placement pattern P.A.C.T. tracks statewide, arriving in Oakland County. Mar 12 minutes · Jan 12 minutes
Proven 4 — Springfield's Moratorium Status
Extended through Dec 10, 2026 (Resolution 2026-09) — it did not lapse
The 180-day moratorium adopted in early January 2026 was amended and extended through December 10, 2026 at the July 9 board meeting, with a revised draft ordinance headed to the Planning Commission. Developer interest is real but pre-application: an ~80-84 acre site off Andersonville Rd, and the township's own Q&A concedes "a general inquiry, not a formal application." The township has no municipal water or sewer. Jul 9 minutes · township Q&A
Proven 5 — Wixom: Sue, Then Watch the Council Retreat
Federal suit Aug 7 → adopted setback 1,500 ft, not the 2,640 ft legal review had reached → city now says its own new ordinance moots the case
Wixom Industrial One LLC (developer, Sansone Group partner, ~519,400 sq ft "Midwinds" project) sued the City in federal court Aug 7, 2026 — after the city's own Aug 3 legal-review draft had raised the setback to 2,640 ft (half a mile). At the Aug 25-26 adoption vote, held while the suit was pending, Council did not hold that line: three of seven members wanted to keep the half mile and were outvoted; the final adopted setback is 1,500 ft, with Councilman Peter Behrmann and Mayor Beagle saying on the record they picked the smaller number specifically because it felt more defensible in court. On Sept 2 the City moved to dismiss the suit as moot, arguing its own new ordinance supersedes the site plan the suit was about — unresolved as of this writing. One of the developer's 29 litigation exhibits is a Dec 11, 2025 memo from Carlisle|Wortman, the city's own paid planning consultant — the firm's advice to the city is now evidence in a private suit against it. Case: Wixom Industrial One, LLC v. City of Wixom, No. 2:26-cv-12754, E.D. Mich. (Judge Stafford). WHMI · Detroit News · docket · City ordinance page
Proven 6 — Project Flex Beat the Moratorium
Approved before Lyon Township paused anything — with its own DTE substation coming
Verrus (Sidewalk Infrastructure Partners — Alphabet, StepStone, Ontario Teachers) won Planning Commission site-plan approval in September 2025 for ~180 acres / ~1.8M sq ft on Walbridge land on Milford Rd. The township's March 2026 moratorium covers only NEW projects and does not touch it. DTE is "planning a substation to accommodate all energy requirements." A recall targets all seven trustees. Township FAQ · Detroit News
Proven 7 — The Real Connector Is the Grid
Wixom and Project Flex sit ~3 miles apart on DTE substation capacity — not a corporate conspiracy
Wixom's council named the former Ford plant's underused DTE substation as what attracts developers; Project Flex gets a new DTE substation. The two sites are roughly 3 miles apart with no corporate link between their developers (Sansone is a Missouri spec-industrial firm; Verrus is Alphabet-backed infrastructure). Data centers cluster where interconnection is cheap and fast. That is testable: DTE's load-interconnection activity runs through the MPSC — the same venue where the AG and Sierra Club are already challenging DTE's Oracle contracts. Follow the substations, not the LLCs.
Proven 9 — The "Working Model" Admission
CWA's own memo: the ordinance is "based on our firm's working model"
Megan Masson-Minock's June 15, 2026 memo to the Springfield Planning Commission, on full Carlisle/Wortman letterhead: the draft amendments "were originally drafted by Giffels Webster and then supplemented by Carlisle Wortman, based on our firm's working model and input from the law firm Rosati, Schultz, Joppich, Amtsbuechler." The first written, first-party acknowledgment that the statewide CWA template exists — and the draft carries its fingerprints: PUE ≤ 1.2, 90% renewable, and 50/40 dBA noise limits identical to Pittsfield's CWA draft; a definition nearly verbatim from Superior Township's; 8 a.m.–6 p.m. generator testing; and the umbrella super-category under its third name ("Large-Scale Industrial Development" = Allen Park's "HRDF" = Saline's "HEID"). Fairness note: this draft sits at the strong end of CWA's range — Springfield has no live application, which is exactly when the template gets teeth. Sources: June 15 memo in the July 28 PC packet; revised draft (Aug 18 memo, pp. 12–33) in the Aug 25 PC packet.
Proven 8 — August 25 Convergence
Two decisive meetings, same night
Monday Aug 25, 2026: Wixom City Council holds the second reading and adoption vote on its weakened ordinance, while the Springfield Township Planning Commission takes up its Draft Data Center Zoning Ordinance Amendments, the Sheetz Final Site Plan (8500 Dixie Hwy), and Levy mine updates — all on one agenda, all now under CWA's planning review. Bodies in both rooms matter. Springfield PC agenda
Proven 10 — The Broker & the Vehicle
The Springfield "general inquiry" is a national data-center broker and a purpose-built land LLC
The township minutes describe the December 2025 approach as "a general phone call." The public record is more specific. The broker who attended the pre-application meeting is a Senior Director of Cushman & Wakefield's national Data Center Solutions practice — the channel hyperscalers and institutional investors use for site selection, not a local agent. And the 84-acre site itself (8300 Andersonville Rd) is held by 84 Andersonville LLC — a single-purpose company formed December 18, 2023, named for the acreage and the road, whose parcel taxes recently uncapped (the signature of a recent ownership transfer). None of that is an accusation; it simply means the "inquiry" sits on a purpose-formed land vehicle and a national data-center brokerage, two years in the making. Sources: Michigan LARA; Oakland County Property Gateway; Springfield Township pre-application minutes, Dec 15, 2025.
Proven 11 — A Corridor-Wide Land Position
The same owner holds data-center-adjacent land across the northern-Oakland transmission belt
Public business-registry records show the owner of the Springfield parcel is a local land investor whose holdings run across the same northern-Oakland corridor now dense with data-center activity — including entities for Springfield, Holly, and Groveland land (a "Groveland Real Properties" LLC among them). That matters because of what a resident told the Springfield Planning Commission on June 23, 2026: two data-center developers are courting Groveland's ITC transmission-line parcels "very close to Springfield." So the picture is a single investor quietly positioned along the power corridor ahead of the wave — legal and ordinary in itself, but worth watching. Not yet confirmed: whether the investor's Groveland land is one of the parcels those developers are after. Sources: Michigan LARA resident-agent records; Springfield PC minutes, June 23, 2026.
Proven 12 — The Grid Test (DTE)
No Oakland data center has reached the utility-contract stage — yet
Per DTE's own investor disclosure, the utility holds exactly two data-center special contracts statewide: Saline Township (approved Dec 18, 2025 — the ~1.3 GW Oracle/OpenAI project, now under an AG reopen motion) and Van Buren Township (Wayne County), pending before the MPSC. Together they are worth "nearly $9 billion" to DTE through 2045. The takeaway for Oakland County: no local project — not Wixom, not Lyon's Project Flex, not Springfield — has reached that utility-contract stage yet. That makes the MPSC docket an early-warning tripwire: the day an Oakland project files a DTE special contract, it has stopped being theoretical. Sources: DTE Energy investor release (2026); MPSC news releases.
Proven 13 — Groveland's Quiet Preparation
A non-CWA township writing data-center rules before any application arrives
Groveland Township — directly north of Springfield, and planned by Wade Trim, not Carlisle/Wortman (so this is a separate thread from the CWA-template pattern) — ran a January 2026 master-plan visioning workshop that explicitly added standards for data centers, battery storage, commercial solar, and commercial wind. Townships don't proactively write data-center rules unless someone has been asking, which lines up with the resident testimony about developers courting Groveland land. Groveland appears on no public data-center tracker yet — the activity is early and quiet. Sources: The Citizen, Jan 17, 2026; Groveland Township Planning Commission.
🚩 Unproven allegations — verify before repeating
Unproven 1 — Golf-Course Data Center
"A perfect spot for a DC behind Lyon Oaks"
Lyon Oaks IS a county-owned park/golf course near the Wixom site — that part is true. But no application, proposal, appraisal, or record of any data center there exists, and the claimed mechanism is false: a county commissioner cannot rezone anything (zoning belongs to Wixom and Lyon Township), and parkland disposal requires a public BOC vote. What would prove it: FOIA Oakland County Parks and Economic Development for any Lyon Oaks appraisal, disposal study, or developer inquiry.
Unproven 2 — The Golf-Course Sighting
"We saw Woodward, Kolo, and Verrus at the golf course"
Currently an undocumented eyewitness claim ("Kolo" unidentified). Given Proven 1-2, it is worth taking seriously — but only documented: what would prove it: the date, who was present, what was observed, written down now; then FOIA county emails and calendars for Verrus, Sidewalk Infrastructure, or Project Flex contacts. Undocumented, it is a rumor that discredits the documented material next to it.
Checked — False 3 — "Nuclear Available"
No nuclear power to tap near Lyon/Wixom
The nearest nuclear plant is DTE's Fermi 2 in Monroe County, roughly 60 miles away. Natural gas transmission in the corridor is plausible; "nuclear available to be tapped" is not a real feature of these sites and should be dropped from the story entirely.
Reconciled 4 — "Three Developers in the Minutes"
Likely origin found: one in Springfield + two next door in Groveland
No Springfield minutes say three developers are courting Springfield. What the record shows: one documented Springfield pre-application meeting (Dec 15, 2025 — Stephen Sevenich of Cushman & Wakefield, for an unnamed client, on the 84-acre Andersonville Rd parcel), plus Groveland Township resident Steve Ford telling the June 23, 2026 Springfield PC on the record that two data-center developers are courting Groveland's ITC-transmission-line parcels "very close to Springfield." One + two = "three in the area," garbled into "three in Springfield." The same June 23 minutes capture a record-vs-reality moment: Chair Galley called the developer contact "a general phone call," and resident Kara Okonewski corrected him on the record by citing the pre-app minutes. Remaining check: December 2025 board/PC minutes for the original moratorium resolution number. Sources: Dec 15, 2025 pre-app minutes · June 23, 2026 PC minutes (in the July 28 packet).
Unproven 5 — The "$6 Billion" Remark
Independence Twp official quoting a Springfield offer
Secondhand hearsay as it stands. What would prove it: the person who heard it on record (name, date, setting), or the statement appearing in Independence Township minutes or a recording. Until then, do not repeat the number.
Unproven 6 — "Sansone Kicked Out of York Twp"
No record found
Searches found no coverage or record of Sansone Group being rejected in York Township (Washtenaw Co.). What would prove it: York Township PC/board minutes or a local news item naming Sansone. Also note the corrected developer structure: Wixom Industrial One LLC is the entity, Dennis Griffin the developer, "Pleasant Ridge" his home city (not a company), Sansone the partner — one team, not three.
Unproven 7 — Mine ↔ Data Center Link
"The gravel mine is for the data centers"
The Levy/Burroughs mine is real (304 acres — 244 Springfield + 60 Rose Twp — at 6651 Ormond Rd, not 422 acres as circulated), and gravel generically feeds construction. But no document links this mine to any data center project. Keep them as two fights sharing a township and a planner — welding them together without evidence hands opponents an easy debunk. Sierra Club
Reconciled 5 — The Lyon "Aug 5 Debrief"
The extension is now confirmed by name; the "Aug 5," "unanimous," ethics-policy, and golf-outing specifics still aren't
A forwarded debrief claimed a unanimous Aug 5 moratorium-extension vote, a resident ethics-policy push, and two trustees called out over a sponsored golf outing with project beneficiaries. A bylined Sept 8, 2026 local-news piece (Livingston Post) independently confirms the Board of Trustees did extend the moratorium "in early August 2026" — so the core claim was right — but does not pin down Aug 5 specifically or "unanimous," and the ethics-policy push and golf-outing conflict callout still have zero corroboration anywhere. Treat the extension as real; keep the other three specifics flagged pending a direct pull of Lyon Township's own posted minutes (site blocks automated review — pull manually or call (248) 437-2240).
