⚑ Eight recurring patterns — one firm, one playbook
Pattern 1 — Placement
CWA is positioned before the developer arrives
In every live-application community, CWA holds the planning role before the developer surfaces. Saline Twp: Auerbach already retained when RD Michigan arrives. Berlin: Kreps hired Sept 2025 — and raises data centers at her first PC meeting, offering to draft a "template ordinance," months before CNBC names Microsoft. Allen Park: Auerbach advising when Solstice applies. Wixom: CWA holds the Master Plan and planning contract a full year before Sansone's waiver request. Honesty note: CWA serves so many SE Michigan communities (Troy and Berkley show no data center activity at all) that placement alone proves little — what's probative is what the planner does once the developer lands, which is what the rest of this strip documents.
Pattern 2 — Circular Authority
CWA writes the guidebook CWA planners then cite
Auerbach co-reviewed the U of M Data Center Guidebook (Feb 6, 2026). The guidebook recommends 8 protections Auerbach refused to require in Allen Park. He then taught it statewide at the MAP February 2026 webinar — disseminating to planners like Kreps (Berlin) the same framework he failed to follow himself. CWA writes the standard → CWA ignores the standard → CWA promotes the standard as authoritative. And the guidebook cites the Saline consent judgment as an example of "enforcing community benefits" (footnote 66) — while that judgment was under active legal challenge as the vehicle that overrode a 5-2 denial. The loop doesn't just spread the standard; it launders the failure into a statewide best practice.
Pattern 3 — Shared Numeric Template
Corrected Aug 1, 2026: same numbers, not a noise loophole
An earlier version of this card claimed Berlin Township's draft left cooling equipment unregulated at a 65 dBA generator-only cap. A direct read of the actual ordinance text disproves that: it caps "backup generators and cooling equipment" together at 65 dBA, with no loophole. What IS confirmed, per the full-text comparison last updated Aug 4, 2026: 9 template matches across ~40 Michigan municipalities checked — near-identical "Purpose and Intent" language and matching numeric standards (60% max lot coverage, 8am–6pm generator testing hours, renewable-energy and PUE targets) reused and tuned per site, not independently reinvented each time. Wixom is the control case: same firm, same months, zero template fingerprints — proving the template is a choice, not a house style. NEW 2026-09-05: Putnam Township (Livingston Co.) is a second control case, found while inventorying master plans — CWA's Joe Pezzotti holds the township's master-planning contract, but the May 13, 2026 data-center/cryptocurrency-mining ordinance draft there was presented by the township's own in-house Zoning Administrator, Ashley Cepeda, with no CWA authorship anywhere in it. See the vault's CWA-Ordinance-Pattern-Comparison note for the full analysis.
Pattern 4 — Developer Law Firm
Dykema Gossett follows CWA across jurisdictions
Alan Greene / Dykema Gossett PLLC appears in both townships where a hyperscaler approached a CWA-planned community: Saline Township (RD Michigan/Related Digital) and Berlin Charter Township (May 2026 letter to the Planning Commission, almost certainly for Microsoft). In Saline, Greene didn't just represent the developer — he DRAFTED the consent judgment the township signed, including the 16-day State Tax Commission filing deadline that preceded the falsely certified Sept 10 "public hearing," and he was present at that Sept 10 meeting. (Stated precisely: this is a two-township pattern — other developers in this set used other firms, Warner Norcross for Solstice and Barnes & Thornburg for UM.)
Pattern 5 — Cross-Jurisdiction Network
One CWA planner, multiple townships simultaneously
Laura Kreps (CWA, AICP) is confirmed as Zoning Administrator for Berlin Charter Township (Monroe County) and Planning/Zoning Consultant for Sumpter Township (Wayne County) at the same time. Sumpter Twp lists CWA on its website as "Township Planning/Zoning Consultant"; Kreps is documented presenting at their planning commission meetings on solar farms, site plans, and use permits. This cross-county presence gives a single CWA consultant simultaneous influence over zoning in two jurisdictions — and creates a single point of contact for developers who need a path through both Wayne and Monroe County municipalities. The Kreps arrangement is one instance of the firm's wider structure: Ben Carlisle's name sits on the letterhead as President while associates are the actual on-the-ground presence — Sally Elmiger in Ypsilanti Twp, Matteo Passalacqua in Wixom, Megan Masson-Minock in Berkley, Marin/Montagno rotating in Salem Twp.
Pattern 6 — Governance Capture
Board members profit from votes the planner enables
In Berlin Charter Township, the Masserant family holds three board seats (Neil Masserant, Debbie Masserant, and a third Masserant candidate in 2024) while their parents' agricultural land awaits rezoning. Trustee Neil Masserant and Trustee Kellie Long are required to recuse from the October 2025 consent judgment vote. The planner (Laura Kreps/CWA) operates throughout this process without flagging or challenging the governance structure. Nine months after the rezoning passes, a Masserant Rd parcel sells for $110,000. A planner whose role is to serve the public interest is professionally obligated to notice and document concentrated governance conflicts — not to accommodate them. 2026 update: three departures in ten weeks — Treasurer Debbie Masserant's resignation letter (May 26 agenda), Trustee Sorovetz's resignation (June 8 special meeting), and Neil Masserant's resignation from the Planning Commission (July 20, keeping his Trustee seat). Timing noted; no confirmed causal link.
Pattern 7 — The Consent Judgment Instrument
Rezonings by settlement, foreclosing citizen remedies
October 2025: two CWA townships rezone by consent judgment within 27 days — Saline Twp (overriding a 5-2 PC denial; entered Oct 15) and Berlin (the Masserant family land; Oct 14/27). The choice of instrument is itself the tell: a consent judgment bypasses the public-hearing record and the citizen referendum rights an ordinance rezoning carries. Saline's — drafted by the developer's own attorney — also pre-committed a future discretionary tax vote ("shall approve," confirmed on tape by the township's own attorney the day of the IDD vote) and imposed the 16-day state-filing deadline that preceded the falsely certified hearing. Ten months later the same judgment was invoked to force a 5-0 reversal restoring Oracle's full $43.1B abatement basis under threat of "consequential damages... in excess of a million dollars." Signed once under pressure; extracting concessions ever since.
Pattern 8 — Record Control
The written record diverges from reality — always in one direction
Across CWA sites the official paper record repeatedly fails to match what video, audio, and FOIA production show happened: Auerbach's review citing a never-enacted 2013 ordinance section; two conflicting sets of Saline's Sept 10, 2025 minutes; an Oct 15 resolution certifying a public hearing that video shows was announced as "not a public hearing"; a vote-tally error in the CWA-authored Allen Park minutes of May 13, 2026; a Berlin planner-rebid whose outcome never appears in any minutes. Every divergence breaks in favor of the application. This is why P.A.C.T. records every meeting: the Aug 12, 2026 State Tax Commission objection shows how a minutes-vs-video divergence converts directly into legal ammunition.

What this timeline supports — and what it doesn't

1. Auerbach was Allen Park's named consultant before Saline's story even began

The 2024 Carlisle/Wortman contract naming Auerbach specifically as Allen Park's Planning Consultant was signed roughly 14 months before Related Digital's first informal appearance in front of Saline Township's Planning Commission (July 8, 2025). He wasn't a planner who "learned the playbook" in Saline and then brought it to Allen Park — he was already Allen Park's guy the entire time Saline's fight was unfolding.

2. The two cases weren't sequential — they overlapped in real time, repeatedly

Four separate dates land an active Saline event and an active Allen Park event on the exact same calendar day: the day the Saline consent judgment was entered and Solstice's plans landed on Carlisle/Wortman's desk (Oct 15, 2025); the day Auerbach spoke with Saline's litigation counsel by phone and Allen Park held its first, most heavily attended postponement hearing (Jan 8, 2026); the day Saline's board tightened its oversight of Carlisle/Wortman and Allen Park's Planning Commission came within one vote of denying Solstice outright (May 13, 2026); and the day Auerbach presented Saline's moratorium/HEID ordinance in the same meeting Saline announced his firm's phase-out, while in Allen Park Fred Frank emailed him the proposal that became Allen Park's own moratorium ordinance (June 2, 2026).

3. A recurring behavioral pattern, not just a recurring person

In both places the same shape repeats: an early planning judgment favorable to the applicant, direct and largely undisclosed-to-the-public contact with the developer's side at the moments that mattered, and Auerbach personally naming or facilitating the developer's next procedural move rather than the city/township's own attorney doing so.

4. The honest limits of the evidence

The Oct. 1, 2025 Teams call is billed under a separate "Analysis/Strategy" category from the consent-judgment drafting itself — it's not proven the call's subject was the judgment. The executed 44-page consent judgment contains zero mentions of Auerbach or Carlisle/Wortman. And Auerbach's June 23, 2026 email to Solstice's own counsel — asserting the moratorium blocks their ZBA appeal — cuts the other way entirely: that's Auerbach helping Allen Park hold the line, not assist the developer. The fair reading isn't "Auerbach always helps the developer." It's that he's a single, largely invisible node sitting inside both communities' most consequential moments, on both sides of the table, more often than a neutral technical consultant's role would predict.

5. What actually connects the two cases — and what a "paves the way" theory would need

The connection this timeline supports is Auerbach personally — not the developers. No ownership or financial link between RD Michigan (Saline's developer) and Solstice LLC (Allen Park's developer) has been found; the two Michigan filings landing one day apart (Aug 25–26, 2025) remain a coincidence, not a confirmed connection. A stronger, better-evidenced claim is narrower: Allen Park's own officials were tracking Saline's outcome in real time. Six weeks after Saline's consent judgment was entered, Mayor Pro Tem Nancy Knick emailed Auerbach directly — "Everything I have been reading on Data Centers have not been favorable. I do not want Allen Park to go through what Saline is going through right now" (Nov 14, 2025) — and Auerbach's reply addressed noise, water, and grid reliability, not the actual mechanism that happened in Saline, where a 4-1 board denial was overridden by a consent judgment two weeks after that vote. There is no evidence the two projects are physically or financially interdependent: Allen Park's facility runs on existing DTE substation capacity, roughly 26 MW against Saline's ~1.4 GW special contract, on different distribution infrastructure about 25 miles apart. What Saline appears to have handed Allen Park isn't power or corporate structure — it's a precedent Auerbach was in a position to carry personally: that a local denial doesn't have to be the end of the process. The single most useful next document to test this further would still be Auerbach's own calendar or detailed timesheet covering July 2025–June 2026.
