Updated 2026-07-25
⚑ Four recurring patterns — same firm, four communities
Pattern 1 — Placement
CWA is positioned before the developer arrives
In all four communities, CWA holds the planning role when a data center proposal lands. Saline: Auerbach already retained. Berlin: Kreps hired Sept 2025, Microsoft surfaces months later. Allen Park: Auerbach advising when Solstice applies. Ypsilanti: CWA's own president is the planner. A developer navigating local zoning benefits from having a sympathetic planner already in the room.
Pattern 2 — Circular Authority
CWA writes the guidebook CWA planners then cite
Auerbach co-reviewed the U of M Data Center Guidebook (Feb 6, 2026). The guidebook recommends 8 protections Auerbach refused to require in Allen Park. He then taught it statewide at the MAP February 2026 webinar — disseminating to planners like Kreps (Berlin) the same framework he failed to follow himself. CWA writes the standard → CWA ignores the standard → CWA promotes the standard as authoritative.
Pattern 3 — Template Gap
Same noise ordinance hole in every community
Both Auerbach (Allen Park) and Kreps (Berlin) shaped noise standards capping backup generator noise at 65 dBA while leaving continuous operational equipment — chillers, cooling towers, HVAC — completely unregulated. These are the 24/7 sources neighbors actually live with. This is not two independent planners making the same mistake. It is one template replicated with the same structural gap that favors the developer.
Pattern 4 — Developer Law Firm
Dykema Gossett follows CWA across jurisdictions
Alan Greene / Dykema Gossett PLLC represented the Saline Township developer (RD Michigan LLC) AND sent a letter to Berlin Charter Township's Planning Commission in May 2026 — almost certainly representing Microsoft. The same developer-side law firm appears in every community where CWA planners are positioned. This is a known practitioner relationship: planning consultant and development attorney who work the same circuits.
