How to Use the Planner Map

A five-minute guide to finding your community, reading what you see, and knowing what the map does and does not say.

Start here: this is a research tool, not a verified directory. It is updated by hand from public records. Always check anything important against the municipality's own documents before you rely on it.

Quick start

  1. Close the welcome notice. Click "I understand, take me to the map."
  2. Find your community. Type a city, township, village, planner name, or planning firm in the search box at the top left. The list and the map narrow as you type.
  3. Click a name in the list (or any dot on the map). The map zooms in and opens a details card.
  4. Read the details card. It shows who does planning for that community, the firm, flags, any data center case notes, research notes, and links to meeting documents where available.
  5. Turn on extra layers from the "Layers" menu on the map (power lines, pipelines, proposed sites, and more). They are off by default so the map stays readable.

Reading the dots and icons

Dot colorWhich planning firm serves that community. The legend at the bottom right shows each color.
Red outline / ⚠An active CWA client.
🔶A former CWA planner now working in-house for the community ("CWA alumni").
⚡The community has a data center case: a proposal, an ordinance, a moratorium, or a lawsuit. The details card explains which.
🔌A DTE Energy build or proposal.
🔋A battery energy storage (BESS) case. Hover the icon for the reason.
✕ / fadedThe planning contract has ended.

Map LayersConnection lines, plus "only" filters to show just data center, DTE, or BESS communities.
Power GridMajor transmission lines and substations (345 kV and up), the DTE/Fermi overlay, ITC/MISO grid queue points, ITC's proposed Copperton routes, and natural gas pipelines. Some of these load live from outside sources and can take a few seconds.

Popups on these layers label each item CONFIRMED or WATCH and name the source.

Common questions

Does a dot mean a data center is coming to my town?

No. Most dots simply show who does that community's planning. Only the ⚡ icon means there is a data center case, and the details card says what kind.

Why is my community marked CWA? Is that bad?

It means CWA is the community's contracted planner. That alone is not wrongdoing. The map tracks it because CWA has drafted data center ordinances for many communities, and residents may want to compare those drafts. Read your own community's documents and decide for yourself.

Being near a power line or on a watch list means a project is planned?

No. Grid layers and watch lists show where conditions could attract interest. They are not evidence of a proposal.

My community says "Unknown." Why?

We could not confirm the planner from public records yet. If you know, please send a correction.

Something is wrong or out of date.

Use the "Submit a Correction" button on the welcome notice. Include your source (a meeting agenda, minutes, or a contract) so the fix can be verified.

The map is slow or blank on my phone.

It holds a lot of data. Give it a few seconds, or use a laptop or desktop for the best experience. Turning off layers you are not using helps.

Where do I find the actual documents?

Many details cards have a "Meeting Documents" section with links. Otherwise, check your community's website for planning commission and council agendas, packets, and minutes.
